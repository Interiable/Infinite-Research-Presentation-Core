## 1.1.3 Samsung Executive Strategic Goals and Objectives for the Hire

The appointment of Mauro Porcini as Samsung Electronics’ first Chief Design Officer (CDO) on April 1, 2025, signals a fundamental shift in corporate strategy [REF-009] [REF-145]. This hire is designed to transition Samsung from an engineering-centric legacy toward a design-led organization where human-centricity serves as a primary business driver [REF-003] [REF-230]. Executive objectives for this role are structured around five strategic pillars: cultural transformation, AI-era modernization, global brand unification, meaning-driven innovation, and cross-divisional integration.

### I. Cultural Transformation: From Engineering to Design-Led
A primary mandate for the CDO is to embed "design thinking" across the entire organizational fabric of Samsung [REF-008] [REF-037]. Historically, Samsung’s culture has been dominated by technical engineering, with design often functioning as a secondary phase [REF-008] [REF-230]. The executive objective is to elevate design into a core strategic function [REF-008] [REF-230].

This shift is driven by the philosophy that "innovation is an act of love," a core belief Porcini is tasked with instilling to ensure products are developed with deep empathy for human aspirations [REF-001] [REF-142] [REF-173]. By reporting directly to the CEO, the CDO ensures that design-led perspectives influence the earliest stages of executive decision-making [REF-205].

### II. Modernizing for the AI Era: The Innovation Formula
Samsung leadership has identified the "AI era" as a critical inflection point requiring a departure from industry-standard "tech minimalism" toward "expressive design" that celebrates human diversity [REF-003] [REF-013] [REF-011] [REF-149] [REF-232]. 

To guide this transition, the CDO utilizes a specific innovation framework: **AI x (EI + HI)** [REF-033] [REF-181].
*   **AI (Artificial Intelligence):** The technological engine [REF-033].
*   **EI (Emotional Intelligence):** Understanding human feelings and connections [REF-033] [REF-181].
*   **HI (Human Imagination):** The creative vision technology cannot replicate [REF-033] [REF-181].

The goal is to ensure that AI integration serves the ultimate objective of "emotion," enhancing the human experience rather than merely automating tasks [REF-033] [REF-181].

### III. Global Brand Unification and Identity
As the first foreign design lead in Samsung’s history, Porcini is tasked with looking beyond internal design culture to establish a consistent, global voice [REF-003] [REF-013] [REF-049]. The executive goal is to bridge the gap between form and function, creating a unified identity that resonates across international markets [REF-049] [REF-155] [REF-182].

This involves managing a global network of approximately 1,500 designers across hubs in Seoul, the United States, Europe, and Asia [REF-091] [REF-158] [REF-218]. The objective is to move away from fragmented design toward a cohesive aesthetic that defines the "next chapter in design innovation" [REF-009] [REF-049] [REF-144].

### IV. Meaning-Driven Innovation
A core strategic pillar is the shift from product-centric to meaning-centric design, summarized by the mantra: **"Form and function follow meaning"** [REF-043] [REF-166] [REF-236]. Every product launch must address three critical dimensions:
1.  **People:** Meeting genuine human needs and aspirations [REF-008] [REF-015].
2.  **Technology:** Leveraging opportunities while addressing technical challenges [REF-008] [REF-147].
3.  **Business:** Navigating the complexities and responsibilities of a global corporation [REF-008] [REF-175].

By focusing on "meaning," Samsung aims to create products that foster deep brand loyalty and long-term value [REF-007] [REF-048].

### V. Cross-Divisional Integration and Execution
The CDO holds centralized authority over the **Device eXperience (DX) Division**, encompassing Mobile eXperience (MX), Visual Display (VD), and Digital Appliances (DA) [REF-017] [REF-181]. The strategic goal is to eliminate "silos" between product categories [REF-009] [REF-230].

By overseeing the design language for smartphones, televisions, and home appliances simultaneously, the CDO ensures a seamless user experience across the Samsung ecosystem [REF-009] [REF-144] [REF-150] [REF-189]. This integrated approach is designed to strengthen overall design capabilities and ensure that innovations, such as mobile AI, are effectively translated across all hardware categories [REF-009] [REF-220] [REF-312].

## 1.3.1 Defining Human-Centric Design: The Porcini Philosophy

The appointment of Mauro Porcini as Samsung Electronics’ first-ever President and Chief Design Officer (CDO) on April 1, 2025, represents a fundamental strategic pivot in the company’s innovation trajectory. ### The "People in Love with People" Ethos
Foundational to this approach is Porcini’s published work, *The Human Side of Innovation: The Power of People in Love with People*, which serves as the theoretical bedrock for his leadership at Samsung. Porcini defines human-centric design as **"a culture based on people in love with people."** This philosophy posits that while data and user insights are necessary, they are insufficient without "caring"—an emotional commitment to the user that transcends traditional market research [REF-053].

Under this framework, innovation is categorized as an **"act of love."** This requires designers to treat end-users as "family"—specifically as parents, children, or friends—to ensure products provide genuine societal value. By prioritizing human value over economic value, the philosophy seeks to address deep-seated human needs, aspirations, and dreams.

### The Three Lenses of Innovation
To operationalize human-centricity within Samsung’s global infrastructure, Porcini utilizes a strategic model consisting of three distinct "worlds" or lenses. This framework ensures that human needs drive innovation while maintaining business and technical viability:

*   **The World of People:** Focuses on human needs, wants, and dreams. It serves as the primary driver, ensuring the "why" of every product is rooted in human experience [REF-147].
*   **The World of Technology:** Views manufacturing and technical capabilities strictly as enablers rather than goals. Technology is leveraged to realize human-centric solutions [REF-175].
*   **The World of Business:** Addresses market viability, strategy, and global distribution. This lens ensures that innovation is sustainable and scalable within a competitive landscape [REF-147].

### The Meaning-First Paradigm: AI x (EI + HI)
A core tenet of the Porcini Philosophy is the mantra that **"form and function follow meaning."** This principle dictates that the aesthetic (form) and utility (function) of a product must be secondary to the significance or "meaning" it holds for the user. 

In the AI era, this is expressed through a specific innovation formula: **AI x (EI + HI)**. 
*   **AI (Artificial Intelligence):** Represents computational power and efficiency.
*   **EI (Emotional Intelligence):** The capacity to understand and respond to human emotions.
*   **HI (Human Imagination):** The creative ability to envision new futures.

By multiplying AI by the combination of EI and HI, technology becomes a tool for enhancing human potential. This approach signals a shift away from "tech minimalism" toward an **"expressive design"** language that celebrates human diversity and identity.

### Framework for Building Brand Value
Porcini’s framework for building brand value at Samsung centers on the transition from a technology-led company to a design-led organization. By embedding "design thinking" across the entire enterprise, Porcini aims to bridge the gap between technical capability and human aspiration. 

Brand value is translated and scaled globally by ensuring that every product—from mobile devices to home appliances—communicates a consistent "meaning" that resonates emotionally with users. This shift from "tech minimalism" to "expressive design" is intended to build long-term brand equity by making Samsung products feel like essential partners in the user's life rather than mere utility tools.

### Strategic Integration at Samsung
Porcini’s philosophy is currently being embedded across Samsung’s **Device eXperience (DX) Division**, covering mobile devices, displays, and home appliances. Reporting directly to the CEO, Porcini oversees a global team of 1,500 designers across hubs in Seoul, the United States, Europe, and Asia. The mandate is to transform Samsung into a design-led organization where human-centricity is the primary driver of all future innovation.

## 2.1.1 Analysis of Technology-Driven Aesthetics and Hardware-Spec Emphasis

The pre-2025 design strategy at Samsung Electronics was defined by a "performance over personality" ethos, prioritizing hardware specifications and functional integration over distinctive aesthetics [REF-011] [REF-016]. This orientation established "tech minimalism" as the formalized design language, where visual form was derived from material innovation and engineering precision rather than purely decorative intent [REF-011] [REF-161].

#### The Framework of Tech Minimalism
Tech minimalism operationalized Samsung’s hardware-centric value proposition into a marketable aesthetic. This language was governed by a foundational formula:

**Hardware Specifications + Minimalism = Functional Elegance** [REF-161]

Under this framework, design served to reduce cognitive load through clean interfaces, restrained color palettes, and geometric consistency, such as uniform bezels and curved edges [REF-016] [REF-161]. The evolution of this strategy occurred across four distinct phases:

*   **1969–1992 (Samsung Electronics Embraces Design):** A foundational period focused on initial minimalism and the adoption of industrial design principles [REF-016].
*   **1993–2004 (The Final Battle Is in the Field of Design):** The strategic integration of design into the core business model [REF-016].
*   **2005–2014 (Design as the Key to the Future):** The peak of hardware-spec-driven design, where technical superiority dictated aesthetic choices [REF-016].
*   **2015–2024 (Design, Standing Firm in the Center of Soft Power):** A transitional phase moving toward expressive design while remaining rooted in functionalism [REF-016].

#### Hardware-Spec Emphasis as a Value Proposition
Samsung’s design strategy treated technical specifications—including screen resolution, battery capacity, and camera optics—as the primary driver of user perception [REF-016]. Design’s role was to facilitate the seamless fusion of these functionalities with the physical hardware. 

In the television market, the integration of QLED technology and 4K resolution was supported by a design language emphasizing visual simplicity, allowing hardware performance to remain the focal point [REF-009] [REF-034]. This approach secured Samsung’s position as the global TV market leader for 19 consecutive years, spanning 2005 to 2024 [REF-009] [REF-034] [REF-040].

In the mobile sector, Samsung’s 2024 recovery of the global smartphone market lead was driven by hardware-spec differentiation [REF-009]. The S24 series utilized design to highlight high-performance components, such as 50MP camera arrays and advanced display panels, reinforcing quality through visible engineering prowess [REF-009] [REF-161].

#### Strategic Implications and the Transition to Expressive Design
While tech minimalism successfully linked hardware specs to market dominance, the "performance over personality" approach eventually necessitated a strategic pivot. By 2024, the reliance on technology-driven aesthetics was identified as a factor in a "personality gap," where products lacked a consistent global voice [REF-004] [REF-161]. 

This led to the appointment of Mauro Porcini as the first-ever Chief Design Officer on April 1, 2025, marking a shift from "tech minimalism" to "expressive design" [REF-009] [REF-016]. This transition aimed to bridge the gap between form and function by embedding design thinking across the Device eXperience (DX) Division, moving beyond the era where hardware specifications alone defined the brand’s aesthetic boundaries [REF-011] [REF-017] [REF-161].

*   **Smart**: Highlighting AI-driven personalization that recognizes individual context and anticipates user needs [REF-197, REF-207].

*   **Nature**: Emphasizing sustainability and the creation of sentimental value to extend product lifecycles [REF-197, REF-207].

### 3. From Technical Utility to Cultural Artifact

## 2.4.3 Defining Emotional ROI: Quantifying Human-Centric Value in Samsung’s New Era

Under Porcini’s leadership, Samsung’s design philosophy has evolved to the mantra: "Form and function follow meaning" [REF-043] [REF-143]. This approach addresses the 2025 market trend of "yearning for authenticity," where consumers increasingly view technology as an extension of personal style and interior identity [REF-346]. 

While Samsung previously prioritized a "black box" aesthetic that minimized a device's presence in the home, the "Design Identity 5.0" framework utilizes two specific pillars to drive Emotional ROI:
*   **Harmony:** Context-adaptive design that integrates into the user's lifestyle [REF-207].
*   **Nature:** The infusion of sentimental value and sustainability into the product's physical form [REF-207] [REF-457].

### The Innovation Formula: AI × (EI + HI)
To operationalize Emotional ROI, Samsung utilizes a proprietary innovation formula designed to differentiate its products in an increasingly algorithmic market [REF-033] [REF-181]:

**AI × (EI + HI) = Meaningful Experience**

*   **AI (Artificial Intelligence):** Foundational technology providing functional automation [REF-033].
*   **EI (Emotional Intelligence):** The ability of the product to resonate with human feelings and needs [REF-181] [REF-207].
*   **HI (Human Intelligence and Imagination):** The creative input ensuring technology remains human-centric [REF-033] [REF-207].

## 2.4.4 The Cultural Relevance Index: Mapping Samsung’s Design to Global Socio-Cultural Shifts

The Cultural Relevance Index is the evolution of a methodology Porcini perfected during his tenure as PepsiCo’s first Chief Design Officer [REF-002] [REF-183]. ### Mechanism and Infrastructure
The index translates qualitative regional trends into actionable design data through a global infrastructure of 1,500 designers operating across eight international hubs, including Seoul, Milan, London, and locations in the USA and Japan [REF-091] [REF-140] [REF-214].
